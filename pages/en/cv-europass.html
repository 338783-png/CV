European CV: how to lay it out

To apply in France, Italy, Belgium, Germany or elsewhere in the European Union, a clear, standardised layout reassures the recruiter. The Europass format, created by the European Union, is the reference.

The expected structure

  1. Personal information: name, address, phone with international code, e-mail.
  2. Position sought: the exact job title from the advert.
  3. Work experience, from the most recent to the oldest: dates, position, employer, city, main tasks.
  4. Education and training, in the same order.
  5. Skills: languages, digital tools, driving licence.

Language levels: from A1 to C2

In Europe, language ability is expressed with the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR):

  • A1, A2: basic user, you get by in simple situations;
  • B1, B2: independent user, you can work and follow a meeting;
  • C1, C2: proficient user, you express yourself fluently and precisely.

Your mother tongue is listed separately, without a level. If you hold a language certificate (DELF, DALF, TOEFL, IELTS, CILS…), mention it with your score.

Photo, age, family situation?

Customs vary from one country to another. In the United Kingdom and Ireland, people include neither a photo nor a date of birth. In France and Italy, the photo is optional. If in doubt, leave these sections empty: a CV without a photo is never rejected for that reason, and the law forbids European recruiters from sorting applicants on these criteria.

Qualifications obtained outside Europe

Write the original title, then its equivalent in brackets, for example “Licence (three-year degree, equivalent to a Bachelor's)”. For some professions, official recognition of the qualification is required: ask the ENIC-NARIC centre of the country you are applying to.
